# Why battery packs ship by sea

Most large lithium-ion packs still cross oceans on container ships, not planes. The reason is less about cost than about rules: a 60 kWh pack is far over what air freight allows for a single battery, and getting a special permit takes weeks of paperwork at both ends.

From the day an order is confirmed, a pack spends about two weeks in final testing, a week waiting for a sailing, and three weeks at sea and in customs. That is where the familiar six weeks comes from.

What builders can do: order the long-lead parts first, book the dock early, and keep spare packs on site, because a damaged pack does not ship back quickly.

None of this is new, but it is easy to forget when a build week is set by the calendar rather than by the supply chain.
